About / 02

Curiosity, made practical.

The best engineering begins with a clear question—and improves through testing, iteration, and collaboration.

I'm a biomedical engineering student drawn to problems where engineering can meaningfully improve human health. I'm interested in how devices, models, materials, and data can help us understand biological systems and create better tools for care.

My approach is to break complex problems into testable questions, understand the constraints, and build a solution that can be evaluated. I value clear documentation, careful analysis, and designs grounded in evidence—not just ideas that look good on paper.

As I develop my experience, I'm looking for opportunities to contribute to biomedical research, medical device development, and interdisciplinary teams that bring engineering closer to clinical needs.

Principle / 01

Start with the need

Understand the user, the biological context, and the problem before selecting a solution.

Principle / 02

Test the assumptions

Use measurement, analysis, and iteration to understand whether a design performs as intended.

Principle / 03

Design responsibly

Consider safety, usability, accessibility, and the limitations of the available evidence.

Principle / 04

Communicate clearly

Make methods, results, and trade-offs understandable to both technical and nontechnical audiences.
